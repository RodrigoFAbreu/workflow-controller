## Goal

Close [#271](https://github.com/RodrigoFAbreu/workflow-controller/issues/271). FL1 made each shape of the functional checklist recogniser (`controller/functional_review.py`) recognise all of its items or refuse. The shapes are still read one at a time: `_flows_of_lines` lets the first of the table, bold, heading and numbered shapes that finds a flow decide, and never looks at what the other shapes recognised. Two examples from the FL1 reviews read as a smaller checklist with no word said:

1. `### A. One` (text) followed by `**B. Two.** x` reads as the one flow `B`. The heading flow `A` is not run.
2. A heading checklist in which one nested case table has an old-form row (`| 1 | ... |`) and a table-less heading `C` that holds its instructions as prose reads as the flows of the table. `C` is not run.

One concern: **when a shape has decided, what happens to a flow another shape recognised**. After this milestone such a flow either lies in the text of a flow that was read, or the checklist is refused naming it. No new setting, option, exit code or gate; the refusal keeps its code and remedy; plain numbers and single letters keep working; flow ids, the label grammar and the order of the four shapes do not change.

## What exists

- `_flows_of_lines(lines, section_level)` (FL1, D7) finds the table rows, then the items of the bold, heading and numbered shapes. Each finder returns a `_Found` with its recognised `items` (label, text), the line `spans` of their text and its `unrecognised` candidates. The table decides when any recognised row is old-form (`_OLD_LABEL_RE`) or when no other shape found an item; otherwise the first of bold, heading and numbered with an item decides, after FL1's checks of the deciding shape alone (its own unrecognised candidates, and a recognised table row outside its spans).
- `_partly_recognised(shape, recognised, bad)` builds the FL1 refusal from `_unrecognised(...)`: code `checklist_unrecognised`, the evidence `section`, `reason`, `shape`, `recognised`, `total`, `unrecognised`, the four shapes and the remedy. `controller/job.py` reads only whether `evidence["recognised"]` is non-zero (`_FunctionalPlan.partial`) to choose the gate text's lead.
- A numbered item, a heading or a bold paragraph that another shape also reads is common in real checklists, because the shapes overlap in the text: a numbered setup list before the flows, a numbered list inside a flow, a case table inside a flow, a heading or bold paragraph that holds the case table whose rows are the flows. The measurement below found four checklists in the repository's history in which more than one shape recognises something.

## Decisions

Each decision states one principle.

- **D1. A flow another shape recognised is accounted for by position, or the checklist is refused.** After the deciding shape has passed its own checks, every recognised item of the bold, heading and numbered shapes that did not decide is compared with the flows read (a table that did not decide is FL1's loose-row check, unchanged). The **flow-text span** of an item is the line span its shape returns for it today (FL1, D7); a table row's span is its own line. An item is **accounted for** under one of two positions, and by nothing else:
  - **nested**: the item *starts* on a line inside the span of a flow that was read (a numbered list inside a bold paragraph, a case table inside a heading flow). A line such as `**Expected.**` inside a flow is not an item of any shape (it has no flow label), so it is never a candidate. Its text is part of that flow's text and is passed to the tester with it;
  - **holding the rows**: the deciding shape is the **table**, and the item's span shares a line with a row that was read (a heading or bold paragraph that holds the case table whose rows are the flows). The rows are the cases of that item, and its prose is the cost FL1 accepted and keeps (it is not passed to the tester, as today).

  An item that starts before or after every flow read and is not a table's holder would not run: the checklist is refused. This includes an item that *begins before* a flow of the bold, heading or numbered shape and runs into it, because that item has text of its own that is not part of the flow read: the heading `### A. One` with its prose, ended only by the next heading, ahead of `**B. Two.** x` (the first example) shares a line with `B` and is still a dropped flow. A numbered list in the prose of a table's holder (`### A. Login` with steps `1.`, `2.`, then an old-form case table) starts inside the span of the holder, which is not a flow that was read, and shares no line with a row, so it is refused naming the steps: this is intended (LMPR-R6-O2), because the holder's prose is not passed to the tester and the steps would not run; the remedy is to write them as bullets or prose, or to put them under `### Setup`. The position is stated once; there is no list of exceptions beyond D2. The cross-shape check has candidates only for the shapes that did not decide and that precede or follow the decider in the fixed order, so it is reachable as follows: with the **table** deciding, a bold, heading or numbered item; with the **bold** deciding, a heading or numbered item; with the **heading** deciding, a numbered item (the bold shape found nothing, or it would have decided); with the **numbered** shape deciding, neither the bold nor the heading shape found an item, so there is no candidate and the only other recogniser, a table that did not decide, is FL1's loose-row check, unchanged. R1 therefore promises a cross-shape refusal for the table, bold and heading deciding shapes only. The rule is the same for every deciding shape except the one named clause of the table holder, and every refusal test of CP1 states which of these positions its item has.
- **D2. A region the checklist marks as not holding flows is never a candidate.** The checklist's own non-flow sections are the words `setup`, `test data`, `known limitations` and `out of scope`: the words of `NEVER_FLOWS` and `_SECTION_END_RE` without `flows`. `flows` is left out on purpose (LMPR-R4-001): `### Flows` and `**Flows.**` are where a checklist conventionally lists its flows (at least nine completed checklists use `### Flows`, for example numbered flows `1.` to `5.` in `docs/milestones/completed/workflow-controller-gen1-correctness-hardening.md`), so the text under them is a candidate region like any other. `NEVER_FLOWS` keeps `flows` for its own job, which this milestone does not change: `_heading_found` does not read a `### Flows` heading as a heading flow, and `**Flows.**` still ends a bold flow in `_SECTION_END_RE`. A heading below the section's own whose title starts with one of the four words (`### Setup`, `### Test data`, `### Known limitations`, `### Out of scope`) is a non-flow region up to the next heading of the same or a higher level; a bold line that opens one of them (`**Setup.**`) is a non-flow region up to the next heading, the next bold labelled paragraph, or the next bold line that `_SECTION_END_RE` matches (any section-opening bold line: `flows` is not a region word, but `**Flows.**` still ends a region, as it ends a bold flow in `_paragraph_found`; LMPR-R5-001), **or the next bold line whose first word is `Flows`, case-insensitively, whatever follows it** (`**Flows.**`, `**Flows**`, `**Flows and expected results.**`, `**Flows and expected results**`; LMPR-R6-001). `_SECTION_END_RE` alone is not enough: it matches only when the region word is the whole bold label, so it misses `**Flows and expected results.**`, which `docs/milestones/completed/workflow-controller-round-caps.md:14-16` and `workflow-controller-codex-reviewer.md:157-166` write after `**Setup.**`/`**Setup**`, and a region running through that heading would hide their numbered flows `1.` onwards (a silent drop). The narrow rule is chosen over "any bold line after a blank line that is not a region word", which would end a region early at a bold note inside `Setup` (`**Note.**`) and make that note's numbered list a candidate; the narrow rule's cost is that a differently worded flows heading (`**The flows.**`) does not end the region, a case the history does not hold and that a one-line change to the rule covers. A `**Setup.**` line followed by `**Flows.**` (or `**Flows and expected results.**`) and numbered flows therefore ends the region at that line, and the flows under it are candidates (the usual way a completed checklist writes it: `**Setup.**` then `**Flows.**`, for example `docs/milestones/completed/workflow-controller-signalhub-notifications.md:51-57`; the six completed checklists of LMPR-R5-001 have no flow inside a non-flow region). An item of a non-deciding shape that starts inside such a region is not a candidate: a numbered setup list is not a flow. The deciding shape's flows are never affected by a region, and the order of the four shapes does not change. This is needed by a real checklist: the `pr-defect-loop` checklist lists `1.` to `3.` under `### Setup` and then a table of flows `1` to `12`, and without D2 it would be refused naming the setup steps.
- **D3. The refusal says which flow would be dropped and by which shape.** It is raised by `_unrecognised` with the code, the shape list and the remedy sentence unchanged, and a reason of the form `the <deciding shape> shape decided <n> flow(s); <k> item(s) another shape recognised neither start inside one of them nor hold a row of a table that decided, and would not run: `A` (heading), `C` (heading), ... Put each inside a flow of the <deciding shape> shape, or give the section one shape` (the first eight named, then `and N more`). The evidence keeps its keys with these values: `shape` the deciding shape, `recognised` the number of flows read, `total` the sum of `recognised` and the dropped items, `unrecognised` the dropped labels in line order (first 20), and gains `dropped`, a list of `{"shape": ..., "label": ...}` in the same order (first 20). The lead is the FL1 lead "only partly recognised" (`recognised` is non-zero by construction), so `controller/job.py` is unchanged. The check runs after FL1's own checks of the deciding shape, so a partly recognised shape is still refused with FL1's reason first, and only one reason is given at a time.
- **D4. Only recognised flows count, and an accepted checklist reads exactly as before.** A candidate that no shape recognised (a token with a digit that is not a label) stays FL1's concern for its own shape; this milestone does not extend FL1's candidate rule to the shapes that did not decide. The flows returned, their order, their labels and their `flow-N` ids are the deciding shape's, as today: the milestone only turns a silent drop into a refusal. A checklist that is accepted today is accepted after, unless D1 finds a flow it drops.
- **D5. One existing test pins the defect and changes; every other assertion stays.** `test_the_first_rule_that_finds_a_flow_decides` (`tests/test_functional_review.py`) builds a numbered item, a bold paragraph and a table and asserts that one flow is read and the others are not: that is the silent drop of D1. It is replaced by tests that pin the same precedence on inputs where nothing is dropped (the numbered item nested in the bold flow, a setup region) and by the refusals of D1. A prototype of D1 (both positions) and D2 applied to a scratch copy of the base commit and run against `tests.test_functional_review` and `tests.test_functional_lifecycle` failed exactly that one (see D6 for the run on the rule of this revision), so no assertion of the lifecycle suite changes.
- **D6. The rule is measured against history before it ships, and the table is reported, not stored in this plan.** The one-off script (kept out of the repository; its result goes in CP1's report and the pull request, because this plan is a protected plan-stage path whose edit after approval would stale the approval) read the checklist section of every revision of `docs/ACTIVE_MILESTONE.md` (269 revisions in `git log`), the 119 files of `docs/milestones/completed/`, the Manager's `cache-miss-message` checklist and the four vendored fixtures (393 files, 64 distinct sections the base reads without a refusal; a section that only links a file is read from it elsewhere). It compared the flows the base reads with the items the other shapes recognise. The planning measurement, re-run on the rule of this revision (64 distinct sections read without a refusal on the base, the same four with more than one shape, none refused):

  | Rule measured | Newly refused | Which |
  |---|---|---|
  | D1 alone (overlap, no D2) | 1 | `workflow-controller-pr-defect-loop`: the setup steps `1.` to `3.` under `### Setup`, beside a table of flows `1` to `12` |
  | D1 and D2 with "shares a line" for every deciding shape (the rule of revision 1) | 0 | none, but it accepts the first example, so it is not the rule of this plan |
  | D1 and D2 with "starts inside a flow" for every deciding shape | 0 | none |
  | D1 and D2 (the rule of this plan: starts inside, and holding the rows for a table that decided) | 0 | none (so the choice is made on the first example and the case table, not on the history) |

  The four checklists in which more than one shape recognises something, and how they read: `bold_labelled_454ffe7` (bold `A`..`J` with three numbered items nested or in the setup), `workflow-controller-ci-reliability` (bold `A`..`J`, numbered `1`..`10` nested in the flows), `workflow-controller-documentation-reorganisation` (headings `A`..`I`, with numbered items and 56 table rows nested or holding them) and `workflow-controller-pr-defect-loop` (a table `1`..`12`, setup numbered `1`..`3` under `### Setup`). None changes. A new refusal of a historical checklist that was a fine checklist would be a defect of the rule, not of the checklist: CP1 reruns the measurement on the implemented code and if any file differs, the rule is tuned until only a genuinely dropped flow is refused and the file is named with the reason.
- **D7. Start-inside, with the table's holder as the one container.** A rule that accepts any item sharing a line with a flow (revision 1) accepts the first example: a heading is ended only by the next heading, so `### A. One` always contains a bold flow that follows it with no heading between, and the drop the issue names could never be refused. A pure start-inside rule refuses that case, but it also refuses the heading that holds the case table whose rows are the flows (the second example's heading `A`), which is ordinary and whose prose FL1 already does not pass on. The two differ in what the flows are: when the table decided, the flows are the rows and the item holding them is their frame; when the bold, heading or numbered shape decided, the flows are the items' own prose, so an item that begins before a flow and runs into it has prose of its own that is not part of the flow read. Positions follow from that and nothing else: the first example (heading before bold) and its mirror (a heading after the bold flow) are refused; a numbered item after the bold flow's first line is inside its span and is accepted; a numbered item before the first bold or heading flow, outside a non-flow region, is refused. A heading that groups bold flows is refused too (the group heading's text is a flow of its own that is not run); the remedy is to give the heading no flow label or the section one shape. A heading that holds a *labelled* bold sub-paragraph (`### A. One` with `**A1.** check x` inside it) is the same input: the bold shape finds `A1` and decides first, so heading `A` is an item of a non-deciding shape that starts before the bold flow and is refused, the shape named `bold`; the remedy is an unlabelled bold line such as `**Expected.**`, which is no item and leaves the heading `A` as the one deciding flow, or one shape. The history of D6 holds no such checklist.

## Risks

- **A false refusal.** D1 turns a silent drop into a stop on a path every automatic round takes. D2 and the measurement are the guard, and the refusal names the item and the shape, so a person fixes the checklist in one edit (put the item inside a flow or give the section one shape). A checklist written with a new combination of shapes may meet it; the lane's guidance (one shape per checklist) avoids it.
- **A non-flow region that is not marked.** A numbered setup list under a bold line or heading the checklist words differently (`**Preparation.**`, `### Before you start`) is not a region and is refused when a bold flow decides, naming the numbered items. The remedy is to move it under `### Setup` or to use bullets; widening the region words is a one-line change with its own test.
- **A container that is refused.** A heading `### A. Group` that holds bold flows, or a numbered item that begins before a flow, is refused when the bold, heading or numbered shape decided, because its own text is not run (D7). It is refused rather than accepted as a container, which would reopen the first example. The refusal names it, and the remedy is one edit (no flow label on the group heading, or one shape in the section). The history holds none.
- **Out of scope:** CU3c, the tester's prompt and results, and any setting.
