## Goal

Close [#227](https://github.com/RodrigoFAbreu/workflow-controller/issues/227). `controller/functional_review.py` reads the flows of the "Functional review checklist" section. It has two defects of one mechanism:

1. **A label of more than a single letter or one or two digits is not recognised.** `_LABEL` is `[A-Za-z]|\d{1,2}`, so `F1`, `3a`, `6.1` and `6b-split` are not labels. A table whose rows are all `| F1 |`, `| F2 |` is refused as `checklist_unrecognised` (the Manager's `cache-miss-message` and `http-read-test-strength`).
2. **A shape that recognises some of its rows drops the others without a word.** `_table_flows`, `_paragraph_flows`, `_heading_flows` and `_numbered_flows` keep what matches and skip what does not, and `read_flows` returns the list. A checklist of nine flows of which three have a label the parser knows is read as a complete checklist of three flows, the tester runs three, and the round is recorded complete. This is the dangerous one: no stop, no message, evidence for flows nobody ran.

One concern: **which items of the checklist section are flows, and what happens to an item that looks like one and is not**. No new setting, option, exit code or gate; the tester's results contract, the `flow-N` ids, the four shapes and their order, and the refusal's code, text and evidence keys stay.

## What exists

- `read_flows(text, read_file=None)` returns `list[Flow]` or raises `ChecklistUnrecognised` (code `checklist_unrecognised`). `_flows_of_lines` tries `_table_flows`, `_paragraph_flows`, `_heading_flows`, `_numbered_flows` in that order and the first that finds a flow decides. `_numbered` gives the ids `flow-1`, `flow-2` in order of appearance.
- `_unrecognised(reason, **evidence)` builds the one refusal: the lead "no functional flow can be recognised in the 'Functional review checklist' section: <reason>", the four shapes from `FLOW_SHAPES`, and the remedy. Its evidence is `{"section", "reason", ...}`.
- Callers: `read_flows` is used by the tester step, `review_mode` and `evidence`; `gate_text` for `GATE_CHECKLIST_UNRECOGNISED` repeats the shape sentence for the operator, and `docs/guide/automation.md` states the shapes.
- Two checklists are vendored in `tests/fixtures/functional_checklists/` (bold labelled `A`..`J` and table `A`..`J`) and `tests/test_functional_review.py` pins the label rule in `test_a_label_is_one_letter_or_one_or_two_digits`.

## Decisions

Each decision states one principle.

- **D1. A shape that recognises one item recognises all of them or refuses.** After a shape finds at least one flow, the remaining items of that shape in the section are counted. If any item looks like a flow and was not recognised, `read_flows` raises `ChecklistUnrecognised` with the same code, the same shape list and the same remedy, instead of returning the partial list. "Looks like a flow" is defined per shape and is deliberately narrow, so the prose and sub-labels a flow legitimately contains do not trigger it:
  - **table:** a table counts when at least one of its data rows (every row below the separator) is recognised. In such a table every data row whose cells are not all empty must have a recognised first cell; the others are unrecognised, named by their first cell (`(empty)` when the first cell is empty). A table with no recognised row is not a flow table and is ignored, as today (a case table inside a flow stays a case table); a row that only the widened grammar accepts and that lies inside the flow text of the shape that decides is such a case-table row, not a candidate and not a flow, unless the table itself decides (D7);
  - **bold paragraph, heading and numbered item:** a line of the shape's own marker form (`**<token>.`, `<token>)` or `:`, `### <token>.`, a top-level `<token>.` or `<token>)`) whose token **contains a digit**, is at most 24 characters of letters, digits, `.` and `-`, and is not a recognised label, is unrecognised. A token with no digit and more than one letter is a word (`**Expected:**`, `**Verify.**`, `**Setup.**`) and is never a flow, so the sub-labels inside a flow and the non-flow sections keep working. For the numbered shape the token has to start with a digit.
  A table continuation row (an empty first cell with other cells filled) is an unrecognised `(empty)` item and refuses the table: today its step is dropped from the flow's text without a word. An optional, qualified flow header (`**Optional, K. Title.**`, which `_SECTION_END_RE` treats as the end of the previous flow) is deliberately **not run and not a candidate**: it is a statement that the flow is optional for the person, it is not a label of the shapes, and it keeps ending the previous flow as today (the vendored `bold_labelled_454ffe7.md` has one). The principle: an item is dropped silently only when it cannot be a label or is the deliberately excluded optional header; anything else that can be a label is either a flow or a refusal naming it.
- **D2. The label is what real checklists use, said exactly.** Measured over every revision of `docs/ACTIVE_MILESTONE.md` (`git log -p`), `docs/milestones/completed/*.md` and the Manager's two checklists, the first cells and bold and heading labels that occur are: a letter (`A`), a letter and one or two digits (`L1`, `B1`, `H10`), one or two digits (`1`, `10`), and one digit and a letter (`7b`); the Manager's `F1`..`F5`; and the lane's guidance adds `6.1` and `6b-split`. The accepted label is therefore, for the table first cell, the bold label and the heading label:

  `[A-Za-z]\d{0,2}` or `\d{1,2}(\.\d{1,2})*[A-Za-z]?`, then optionally one hyphenated qualifier `-[A-Za-z][A-Za-z0-9]{0,15}`.

  | Written | Accepted | Why |
  |---|---|---|
  | `A`, `a`, `7`, `12` | yes | unchanged |
  | `F1`, `L12`, `H10` | yes | a letter and one or two digits |
  | `3a`, `10c` | yes | a number and one letter |
  | `6.1`, `6.1b`, `2.10` | yes | dotted numbers, parts of one or two digits |
  | `6b-split`, `F1-retry` | yes | one hyphenated qualifier |
  | `AB`, `Flow`, `Setup` | no, and not a candidate | a word: more than one letter and no digit |
  | `123`, `F123`, `A1b2`, `6b-` | no, and unrecognised in D1 | has a digit but is not a label |
  | `6 \`step\``, `1 (optional)` | no, and unrecognised in D1 (table) | a first cell with text after the number |

  A bold or heading label needs its closing `.`, `)` or `:`, not followed by a digit. A label with no closing mark (`**6.1 Title.**`) has no marker, so it is neither a flow nor a candidate in D1; the lane's guidance (a plain number or a single letter in the first cell) keeps working as it does. The numbered shape keeps its three-digit number and gains the same digit-led forms (`3a.`, `6.1.`, `6b-split)`); its marker stays a number. The heading `Flow <label>` form takes the same grammar: when the text after `Flow` is a D2 label the flow's label is that label as written (`### Flow 6.1` is `6.1`, so `### Flow 6.1` and `### Flow 6.2` are two distinct labels, where today both are `6`); any other text after `Flow` is read as today (the heading is a flow with the label today's rule captures), and a `Flow` heading is never a D1 candidate because it is already a flow. Flow ids stay `flow-1`, `flow-2` in order of appearance, and a flow's `label` is the label as written.
- **D3. The refusal says what was recognised and what was not.** The partial refusal is raised by `_unrecognised` with a reason of the form `the <shape> shape recognised 3 of 9 items; not recognised: \`3.x\`, \`F1 \`, ...` (the first eight named, then `and N more`), followed by one sentence stating the accepted label (D2). The code, the shape list and the remedy sentence are the existing ones. The lead of the message is the existing one for a checklist with none (`no functional flow can be recognised`) and, for the partial case, `the flows of the 'Functional review checklist' section are only partly recognised`, so the message never says "none" when some were found, **and neither does the operator's stop**: `gate_text` for `checklist_unrecognised` takes a `partial` fact (true when the refusal's evidence has `recognised` above zero; D4) and then opens "the functional review checklist is only partly recognised" instead of "has no flow the Controller can recognise". Evidence keeps `section` and `reason` and adds `shape` (`table`, `bold`, `heading` or `numbered`), `recognised` (int), `total` (int) and `unrecognised` (a list of the names, at most 20). For a checklist with none, when a table was seen and no row of it was a label, the reason also names the table's first cells (the first eight) and the evidence adds `first_cells`; this is the issue's "name the offending first cell" and changes no other case.
- **D4. The words that describe the shapes follow the rule.** `FLOW_SHAPES[0]` becomes "table rows whose first cell is a flow label (`| A | do | expect |`, `| F1 | ... |`)", and the matching sentences in `gate_text` (`GATE_CHECKLIST_UNRECOGNISED`) and `docs/guide/automation.md` say "a flow label" and point at the label rule in the guide. `controller/job.py` reads the refusal's `evidence["recognised"]` where it builds the `_FunctionalPlan` (`job.py:7661`) and carries a `partial` flag with the plan's detail to `_plan_gate_text`, which hands it to `gate_text`; `gate_text`'s lead is "the functional review checklist has no flow the Controller can recognise (<detail>)" when `partial` is false and "the functional review checklist is only partly recognised (<detail>)" when it is true, and the rest of the sentence is unchanged. The `stop(...)` path at `job.py:7915` passes no `partial` and keeps the default. The checklist the planning command writes (`/prepare-functional-review`) already asks for a plain number or a single letter; that guidance stays valid and is not edited here. `docs/common-problems.md` and `docs/guide/troubleshooting.md` name `checklist_unrecognised` among the stops and are changed only where they describe what is recognised.
- **D5. Two existing tests pin the defect and change; every other assertion stays.** (a) `test_a_label_is_one_letter_or_one_or_two_digits` (`tests/test_functional_review.py:145`) asserts that a section with `**AB. no.**`, `**123. no.**` and `**12. yes.**` is read as the single flow `12`: that is the silent drop of D1 (`123` has a digit and is not a label). It is replaced by tests that pin the same three cases under the new rule: `AB` is a word and ignored, `123` is refused naming it. (b) `FlowShapesTest.test_table_rows_below_a_header_and_separator` (`tests/test_functional_review.py:63`) reads a table with the rows `| A |`, `| 2 |` and `| long | no | label |` and asserts two flows; under D1 the non-empty row `long` is unrecognised, so that table is refused. It is replaced by the same table refused naming `long` with `2 of 3`, plus the same table without the `long` row read as flows `A` and `2` with the first flow's text `| A | one | ok |`. Every other test in `tests/test_functional_review.py` was checked against D1 and passes unchanged: the bold tests use `**Setup.**`, `**Flows.**`, `**Test data.**`, `**Expected:**`, `**Verify exit code 0.`, `**Check step 2.`, `**Then run it with option b.` and `**Setup is unchanged afterwards.**` (each label is a word or a multi-word phrase, never a token with a digit), `**Optional, K. Title.**` (O1), the heading tests use `### Flow 2` (a flow already), `### Setup` and `### Notes`, the numbered tests use only digit-led items, the fenced tests are masked, and the two vendored fixtures are covered by D6 (CP1 re-runs them and names any assertion that D1 changes). `tests/test_functional_lifecycle.py`, `tests/test_protocol_job.py` and the scenarios stay as they are and must pass unchanged, except the one gate-text assertion CP2 adds.
- **D6. The rule is measured against history before it ships, and every new refusal is judged by hand.** A one-off script (kept out of the repository, its result recorded in the checkpoint's report and the pull request, never in this plan, which is a protected plan-stage path whose edit after approval would stale the approval) reads the checklist section of every revision of `docs/ACTIVE_MILESTONE.md`, every `docs/milestones/completed/*.md` and the Manager's two checklists with the old and the new `read_flows`, and lists each file whose result differs. The old parser never reports a partial read, so "read partly" cannot be told from the old parser alone and the new parser's own D1 rule is the thing under test. Every file the new parser refuses is therefore **judged by hand**: each unrecognised name is classified as a real flow (the defect, which the refusal is right to name) or as not a flow (a regression: a sub-label such as `**CP1.**` or `**2026-10-01:**`, or a non-flow table row such as `long`), and that classified list is in the report and the pull request. A file the old parser refused and the new one reads is the other allowed difference. Any file the old parser read completely and the new one refuses for a name that is not a flow is a regression: the rule is narrowed until none remains. The script also compares **content**: for every file the old parser read, each non-blank line of every old flow's text must appear in the text of some new flow, and a file that loses a line is a regression whatever the flow count (a shape that wins and discards a flow's instructions is the failure D7 closes, found in review round 3 on `workflow-controller-documentation-reorganisation.md`). A file the old parser read completely and the new one reads differently without a refusal is allowed only as a **named, reasoned difference**, listed in the checkpoint's report and the pull request with the reason. The one known today is `docs/milestones/completed/workflow-controller-settings-and-telemetry.md`, a bold checklist `A`..`P` whose flows `B`, `K` and `L` hold the sub-steps `**B1.**`..`**B6.**`, `**K1.**`..`**K5.**` and `**L1.**`, `**L2.**`. The old parser did not match `B1.`, so those sub-steps stayed in their parent's text and it read 16 flows; under D2 each sub-step is a label (a letter and digits, accepted as `F1` is), so the file reads as 29 flows and `B`, `K` and `L` shrink to their title lines. This is accepted and the grammar is not narrowed: nothing is dropped, every step still runs, once, as its own flow with its own `flow-N` id, and narrowing so that `B1` under `**B.` stays inside `B` would contradict D2's accepted forms and the `**S1:**` test. A test pins this file at 29 flows, with the labels of `B`, `B1`..`B6` in order and `B` reading as its title line. The two Manager checklists, copied from the `workflow-manager` repository, become fixtures.
- **D7. Which shape decides is one ordered rule, and a table row the widened grammar newly accepts belongs to the flow that holds it.** Table-first precedence stays, but the widened first-cell grammar must not change which shape decides for a checklist the old parser read as headings, bold labels or numbered items with case tables inside the flows. `docs/milestones/completed/workflow-controller-documentation-reorganisation.md` is that checklist: the old parser read headings `A`..`I` (eight with nested tables of rows `A1`, `B1`, `D1`.., and `C. Read-through`, whose instructions are prose with no table); read with D2 alone, the nested rows are labels, the table shape wins with 56 flows and `C`'s instructions are dropped with no refusal, since every table is complete. A row is **new-form** when its first cell is accepted by the D2 grammar but not by the old one (`[A-Za-z]|\d{1,2}`), and **old-form** when the old grammar accepts it. The **flow-text span** of an item is the line span of the text its shape returns for that item today: a numbered item ends at the first non-indented, non-blank line (a blank line continues it only when an indented or fenced line follows); a heading ends at the next heading of the same or a higher level, a non-flow heading such as `### Notes` included; a bold paragraph ends at any heading or at a section-end bold paragraph. The order, stated once:
  1. Find the items of the bold, heading and numbered shapes and their flow-text spans. Fenced lines are masked as today.
  2. **If any table row anywhere in the section is old-form, the table shape decides, as today.** Every row of its tables is then subject to D1 and recognised under D2, nested or not: no row is exempt. A case table with rows `1`, `2`, `2a` inside a heading flow is therefore read as the three flows `1`, `2`, `2a`, exactly as the old parser read `1` and `2` and dropped `2a`; this is deliberate (every checklist the old parser read as a table is read as a table), and the cost is that `2a` is no longer dropped.
  3. **Otherwise, if none of the bold, heading and numbered shapes found an item, the new-form rows decide as a table.** A section holding only a standalone `F1`..`F5` table is read as a table.
  4. **Otherwise the first of the bold, heading and numbered shapes (in that order) that found an item decides.** A new-form table row inside a flow-text span of the deciding shape is **nested**: it is in that flow's text by construction, it is neither recognised nor a D1 candidate, and it does not make its table decide. A new-form row outside every flow-text span of the deciding shape refuses the checklist, named in D3's form (the deciding shape, `recognised` its items, the un-nested rows among the unrecognised); the Controller will not guess which of two readings is the checklist, and no row is dropped without a word. A row nested in a span of a shape that did not decide is not nested for this rule.
  Consequences: the documentation-reorganisation checklist reads as headings `A`..`I`, `C` included, with the nested tables staying inside their headings' spans; a heading checklist with a nested `A1` table and a separate table-less heading `C` reads `[A, C]` as today; a numbered setup list (`1.`, `2.`) followed by a blank line and a column-0 `F1`..`F5` table is refused naming the `F1` rows, because they lie after item `2`'s span and the numbered shape decides; a heading flow followed by a non-flow heading `### Notes` of the same level holding a table of `A1` rows is refused naming those rows, because the `### Notes` heading ends the flow's span; an `F1` table before the first heading `### A. Setup` is refused for the same reason. The choice is a pure function of the text, and the order of the four shapes does not change.

## Functional review

Every flow is offline and unit-level (the change is a pure parser), run from the product clone with `TMPDIR` set to a writable scratch directory, `python3 -B` and no `PYTHONPATH=.`. Flows, with a plain number in the first cell: (1) `python3 -B -m unittest tests.test_functional_review` and check the new label and refusal classes ran; (2) read the Manager's `cache-miss-message` fixture with `fr.read_flows` and see `F1`..`F5` as `flow-1`..`flow-5`; (3) read a nine-row table with three label rows and six rows of `Flow 4`-style first cells and see the refusal naming the six and `3 of 9`; (4) read a bold checklist with `**Expected:**` and `**Setup.**` lines inside a flow and see it read whole; (5) read `docs/milestones/completed/workflow-controller-documentation-reorganisation.md`'s checklist section with `fr.read_flows` and see nine flows `A`..`I`, `C` among them with its read-through instructions; (6) `python3 tools/check_docs.py`. The history measurement of D6 needs the repository's full history and is operator-run if the tester's clone is shallow; no flow creates a folder under `/tmp`.

## Risks

- **A false refusal.** D1's candidate rule is narrow (a digit in the token, a marker, or a table that already proved itself) but it is new strictness on a path every automatic round takes. D6's history measurement is the guard, and the refusal names the item so a person fixes the checklist in one edit.
- **A label the grammar leaves out.** A real checklist may use a form this plan did not find (`6 \`step\``, `Flow 3`). A table row with such a first cell is refused only when the table also has a recognised row; a table of them falls through as today. The refusal names the cell, so a new form is a one-line widening with its own test.
- **A bold sub-label with a digit** inside a bold-shaped checklist. Two outcomes, both real: a single letter and digits (`**S1:**`, `**R2.**`) is a label by D2 and silently becomes an extra flow, and a multi-letter token with a digit (`**Step1.**`, `**CP1.**`) is a D1 candidate that is not a label, so the whole checklist is refused naming it. CP1 tests both; D6's hand classification lists the files that have either. The one such file known today, `settings-and-telemetry` (`**B1.**` under `**B.**`), is the named difference of D6.
- **A nested label that is really a flow.** D7 keeps a new-form table row inside the flow that holds it. A checklist of headings whose tables hold the real flows, one per row, would be read as its headings, the way the old parser read it. That is the old behaviour and loses nothing (the rows lie inside the heading's flow-text span, so the tester sees them); a new-form row outside every span refuses instead of being dropped, and the content check of D6 pins that no line is dropped.
- **Out of scope:** CU3c, the tester's prompt and results, and any setting.
